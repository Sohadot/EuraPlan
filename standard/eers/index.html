EP-OUT-05 — Proprietary Standard

European Entry Readiness Standard

EERS defines the minimum conditions for a non-EU company to be genuinely ready to enter the European market. No company-specific scores are shown on this reference page — all dimensions are unassessed until a protocol run.

Entry Gateway Planning Protocol Regulatory Clock
What Readiness Means

Entry-Ready Is Not the Same as Interested

Most non-EU companies that describe themselves as "expanding into Europe" are not entry-ready by the EERS definition. They may have a product, a budget, and an intent. But intent is not a regulatory plan. Budget is not a compliance path. Product-market fit in one geography does not map to entry-readiness in a regulated one.

The EERS defines readiness across eight dimensions. A company scoring below threshold on any single dimension is not entry-ready — regardless of its ambition or market opportunity.

Dimension Reference — Text Detail

Standard Dimension Definitions

  1. Regulatory Mapping

    Has the company identified all EU regulations applicable to its entity type, product category, sector, and intended role (provider, deployer, manufacturer, service provider)? A partial mapping is not a completed dimension. Every applicable regulation must be identified, not just the most visible ones.

  2. Compliance Timeline

    Does the company know the specific enforcement dates that apply to its obligations — not just that a regulation exists, but when each obligation activates? The compliance timeline converts the Regulatory Entry Clock into a company-specific deadline structure.

  3. Country Selection

    Has the target EU entry country been selected with documented evidence — accounting for sector fit, regulatory implementation nuances, talent availability, funding ecosystem, and national execution realities? Country selection made on preference or familiarity is not a scored dimension.

  4. Entity & Legal Structure

    Is the legal structure for EU operations defined? The choice between branch, subsidiary, representative office, or full legal entity has consequences for regulatory obligations, funding eligibility, procurement access, and tax exposure. This dimension requires a decision, not an intention.

  5. Funding Awareness

    Has the company assessed its eligibility for EU funding programmes, grants, innovation support, and public procurement opportunities relevant to its sector and entry country? Funding awareness does not mean the company has applied. It means it has mapped its eligibility and the conditions that determine it. Funding reference node: Horizon Europe Entry Planning Reference.

  6. Product Compliance

    Is the company's product or service mapped against EU requirements for its category? For AI systems, this includes AI Act classification. For connected products, CRA conformity assessment. For health products, MDR obligations. For payments, PSD3 licensing. Product compliance mapping must precede market launch, not follow it.

  7. Risk Classification

    Are the entry risks identified and categorised? This includes regulatory risk (which obligations are most demanding), market risk (which country and sector conditions create uncertainty), and execution risk (where the plan has the least confidence). Risk classification does not eliminate risk. It makes it legible and plannable.

  8. Execution Plan

    Is there a 30/90/180-day entry plan with concrete, sequenced steps — mapped against the regulatory clock, the entity setup timeline, the compliance path, and the funding readiness state? A vision of entering Europe is not an execution plan. An execution plan is a sequenced set of actions with owners and timelines.

Threshold Consequences

What Below-Threshold Means

A company below threshold on Regulatory Mapping is entering Europe with unknown obligations. A company below threshold on Compliance Timeline is operating against deadlines it has not mapped. A company below threshold on Execution Plan is describing an intention, not executing a plan.

The EERS does not grade on ambition. It establishes the minimum conditions for a company to be classified as entry-ready and for its entry to be governed by a plan rather than managed as a series of reactive decisions.
