The Planning Problem

The Four Decisions Every Non-EU Entry Must Make

Most non-EU companies enter Europe without making these four decisions explicitly. That is not an advantage. It is the structure of a preventable failure. Europe does not wait for companies to catch up with its regulatory architecture.

  • 01 — Regulatory Exposure Which EU regulations apply to your company, product, and business model? EU AI Act, GDPR, Cyber Resilience Act, EU Data Act, MDR, PSD3, CSRD — the applicable set is determined by your entity type, sector, and product category, not by your country of incorporation. This must be mapped before any other entry decision.
  • 02 — Country Selection Which EU country do you enter first? The answer depends on your sector, your product, your regulatory profile, your existing relationships, your funding eligibility, and the national implementation nuances of applicable EU frameworks. Country selection is a planning decision, not a preference.
  • 03 — Entity & Legal Structure What legal structure does your EU presence require? Branch office, subsidiary, representative office, EU-based legal entity — the answer affects regulatory obligations, tax exposure, hiring rights, and your ability to access EU funding programmes and tenders.
  • 04 — Entry Sequence In what order do regulatory compliance, entity setup, funding readiness, and market activation happen? Wrong sequencing creates legal exposure and delays that compound. The entry sequence must be planned against the regulatory clock, not after it.
Entry Routing by Situation

Where Does Your Company Start?

Regulatory mapping not yet done

Start with the Regulatory Entry Clock. Identify which EU regulations apply to your entity type, sector, and product before any other step.

Regulations known, plan not built

Use the EuraPlan Entry Planning Protocol. The twelve-step sequence takes you from regulatory exposure to a 30/90/180-day entry plan.

Assessing entry readiness

Apply the European Entry Readiness Standard. Eight dimensions determine whether your company is genuinely ready to enter, or only ready to try.

Exploring EU funding

Funding readiness must be assessed against your sector, country target, and entity type. EU programmes including Horizon Europe, EIC, and ERDF have specific eligibility conditions.

US SaaS or AI company

Your entry must address EU AI Act provider and deployer obligations, GDPR data controller requirements, and EU Data Act implications — simultaneously, before the first EU contract.

GCC or MENA tech company

Entry requires mapping AI governance obligations, data protection requirements, and country-specific market access routes. Funding eligibility depends on entity structure and sector.
