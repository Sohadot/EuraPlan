EP-R-007 — Source Governance

How EuraPlan Sources Its Intelligence

Every regulatory claim, timeline, and funding reference must be traceable to an authoritative source before publication. Source confidence is an operational control — not a marketing label.

SRC-CTRL-01

Why Tier 1 Matters

No regulatory deadline or compliance obligation may be published without a Tier 1 source — official EU legislation (EUR-Lex), Commission guidance, or official national authority publications. Tier 2 and Tier 3 cannot substitute for Tier 1 on regulatory claims. A company acting on uncited regulatory intelligence bears the planning risk; EuraPlan blocks Pending claims from publication.

Tier 1 — Primary Authoritative High risk without Tier 1
Why Source Governance Matters

Regulatory Intelligence Without Source Governance Is Noise

EU regulations change. Enforcement dates shift. Funding programmes open and close. Regulatory guidance gets updated. A company that acted on an outdated regulatory claim is not protected by the fact that the claim was once accurate. EuraPlan's source governance architecture is not a compliance exercise. It is the operational requirement for intelligence that can be trusted.

The Three-Tier Source System

Accepted Source Tiers

Tier 1 — Primary Authoritative

Official EU & National Sources

Required for all regulatory, legal, and compliance claims. Includes official national government sources (e.g. CNIL, BMDV) within Tier 1.

  • Official EU legislation — EUR-Lex (eur-lex.europa.eu)
  • Regulation texts and official recitals as published in the Official Journal of the European Union
  • European Commission official communications, guidance documents, and Q&A documents
  • European Parliament official publications and adopted texts
  • Council of the European Union official documents
  • EU agency official guidance: ENISA, EBA, EMA, ESMA, EDPB, EASA
  • Official national government sources (e.g., Germany's BMDV, France's CNIL, Netherlands' ACM)
  • Official EU funding programme documentation: Horizon Europe, EIC, ERDF, InvestEU portals
Tier 2 — Institutional Reference

Institutional & Multilateral Official

Supporting context and interpretation. Not valid as the sole source for regulatory deadline or compliance obligation claims.

  • Enterprise Europe Network official publications
  • European Investment Bank (EIB) official reports
  • European Investment Fund (EIF) official reports
  • OECD official reports on EU markets
  • IMF official data on EU economies
  • World Bank official data
Tier 3 — Professional Reference

Named Professional Secondary

Contextual framing only. Not valid for regulatory or compliance claims. Must be named, dated, and attributed.

  • Named, dated international law firm regulatory alerts
  • Named, dated Big Four regulatory briefings
  • Named, dated official industry association regulatory updates
Rejected — Not Accepted

Prohibited Source Types

The following are not accepted as supporting sources for any regulatory, compliance, or funding claim under any circumstances:

  • Anonymous or unnamed web content
  • AI-generated summaries presented as source material
  • Wikipedia
  • Undated consulting firm marketing materials
  • Social media posts or forum discussions
Source Confidence Classification

How Claims Are Classified

Every sourced claim carries a confidence classification that determines publication eligibility.

Verified Referenced Pending Deprecated
LevelDefinitionAction
Verified Directly supported by a Tier 1 or Tier 2 source, cited inline May be published
Referenced Supported by a Tier 3 professional reference, disclosed May be published with disclosure
Pending Source required — not yet captured Must not be published until source is captured and verified
Deprecated Source outdated or superseded by a newer official version Must be updated or removed within 30 days
Source Review Cycle

When Sources Are Reviewed

All public pages undergo source review at initial publication, at every content update, on a six-month rolling basis for regulatory claims, and immediately when a governing regulation is amended, replaced, or reaches an enforcement milestone.
