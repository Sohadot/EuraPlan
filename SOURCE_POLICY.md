## 1. Purpose

EuraPlan.com produces planning intelligence that influences real business decisions. Every claim, timeline, threshold, requirement, and statistic must be traceable to an authoritative source.

This document defines which sources are accepted, how they are cited, and what happens when sources are absent or degraded.

---

## 2. Accepted Source Tiers

### Tier 1 — Primary Authoritative
Required for all regulatory, legal, and compliance claims.

- Official EU legislation (EUR-Lex)
- Regulation texts and official recitals
- European Commission official communications and guidelines
- European Parliament official publications
- Council of the European Union official documents
- EU agency official guidance: ENISA, EBA, EMA, ESMA, EDPB, EASA
- Official national government sources (Germany, France, Netherlands, etc.)
- Official EU funding programme documentation (Horizon Europe, EIC, ERDF, InvestEU, etc.)

### Tier 2 — Institutional Reference
Supporting context and interpretation only.

- Enterprise Europe Network official publications
- European Investment Bank (EIB) official reports
- European Investment Fund (EIF) official reports
- OECD official reports on EU markets
- IMF official data on EU economies
- World Bank official data

### Tier 3 — Professional Reference
Contextual framing only. Not valid for regulatory or compliance claims.

- Named, dated international law firm regulatory alerts
- Named, dated Big Four regulatory briefings
- Named, dated official industry association regulatory updates

---

## 3. Rejected Sources

The following are not accepted as supporting sources for any regulatory, compliance, or funding claim:

- Anonymous or unnamed web content
- AI-generated summaries presented as source material
- Wikipedia (orientation only, never citation)
- Personal blog posts
- Consulting firm marketing materials without date and author attribution
- Undated press releases
- Social media posts
- Forum content or discussion threads

---

## 4. Citation Requirements

Every sourced claim must include:

- Source name and type
- Publication or last-updated date
- URL to official source (where available)
- Retrieval date if the source may be updated

If a source URL changes or is removed, the claim must be flagged for re-verification before the next publication cycle.

---

## 5. Confidence Classification

| Level | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Verified** | Directly supported by Tier 1 or Tier 2 source, cited inline |
| **Referenced** | Supported by Tier 3 professional reference, disclosed |
| **Pending** | Source required — content held from publication |
| **Deprecated** | Source outdated or superseded — content must be updated or removed |

No content classified as **Pending** may be published.
Content classified as **Deprecated** must be updated or removed within 30 days.

---

## 6. Regulatory Timeline Claims

Regulatory dates and enforcement deadlines are high-risk claims. Additional rules:

- All deadline claims must cite the specific article and regulation text
- "Expected" or "anticipated" timelines must be labeled as non-final
- If an exact date is uncertain, the page must state this explicitly
- Do not state a deadline as confirmed unless it appears in official regulation text or official Commission communication

---

## 7. Funding Claims

Funding programme amounts, eligibility criteria, and deadlines change. Rules:

- All funding amounts must cite the official programme documentation
- Funding call deadlines must link to the official call page on the EU Funding & Tenders Portal
- If a funding round has closed, the page must state this explicitly
- General funding eligibility descriptions must note that actual eligibility requires individual assessment

---

## 8. Source Review Cycle

All public pages must have source review performed:

- At initial publication
- At every content update
- On a 6-month rolling basis for regulatory claims
- Immediately when a governing regulation is amended, replaced, or has an enforcement date pass
