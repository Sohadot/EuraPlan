EP-OUT-01 — Regulatory Entry Clock

The Regulatory Entry Clock

EU regulations do not apply all at once. They apply on staggered timelines that are determined by your entity type, sector, product category, and AI system role. The Regulatory Entry Clock maps when each applicable regulation begins to govern your company — so you plan backward from enforcement, not react after it.

What the Clock Tracks

Regulatory Timelines Are Not Optional Reading

A non-EU company that knows the EU AI Act exists but does not know which obligations apply to it, in which phase, and from which date, is not ahead of compliance. It is behind it — without knowing by how much. The Regulatory Entry Clock converts regulation texts into a company-specific timeline of obligations, enforcement phases, and planning gates.

Clock Dimensions

What the Clock Maps

  • Applicable regulations Which EU regulations govern your entity type, sector, and product? The set is not fixed — it is determined by what you build, how you deploy it, and where in the EU value chain you sit.
  • Enforcement phases Most major EU regulations apply in phases. Knowing that a regulation is "in force" is not the same as knowing which of your obligations are already active.
  • Planning horizon The gap between an enforcement deadline and the time required to achieve compliance is your actual planning window. Most companies discover this window is shorter than expected.
  • Entity role Under the EU AI Act, the obligations on a provider, a deployer, and a GPAI model developer are distinct. Under GDPR, the obligations on a data controller and a data processor differ. Your role determines your clock.
Established Regulatory Phases — Source: Official EU Regulation Texts

Key EU Regulation Timelines

The following regulatory phases are taken from official EU regulation texts published in the Official Journal of the European Union. Source references are cited by regulation number. Detailed per-company timeline mapping requires full source capture as part of Sprint 1 deep reference page development.

  • EU AI Act
    Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 — EUR-Lex
    Entry into force: August 1, 2024
    February 2, 2025: Prohibited AI practices (Chapter II) apply — 6 months after entry into force (Art. 113.1)
    August 2, 2025: GPAI model obligations and governance framework apply — 12 months (Art. 113.2)
    August 2, 2026: Full application — high-risk AI under Annex III, all remaining obligations (Art. 113.3)
    August 2, 2027: High-risk AI under Annex I (existing EU harmonisation legislation) (Art. 113.4)
  • GDPR
    Regulation (EU) 2016/679 — EUR-Lex
    Full application: May 25, 2018 (ongoing)
    Applies to any company processing personal data of EU residents, regardless of where the company is established. Maximum administrative fines: up to €20 million or 4% of total worldwide annual turnover (Art. 83) — whichever is higher.
  • EU Data Act
    Regulation (EU) 2023/2854 — EUR-Lex
    Entry into force: January 11, 2024
    September 12, 2025: Full application (Art. 50) Applies to manufacturers of connected products and related service providers active in the EU market.
  • Cyber Resilience Act
    Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 — EUR-Lex
    Entry into force: December 10, 2024
    September 10, 2026: Reporting obligations for manufacturers (vulnerability and incident reporting) apply — 21 months after entry into force (Art. 71.2)
    December 11, 2027: Full application — all security requirements for products with digital elements (Art. 71.1)

Source confidence: Verified — dates sourced directly from official regulation texts as published in the Official Journal of the European Union. EU regulations and enforcement timelines are subject to change. Verify current requirements at EUR-Lex before acting.
