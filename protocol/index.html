EuraPlan Entry Planning Protocol

The EuraPlan Entry Planning Protocol is the twelve-step sequence that governs all EuraPlan intelligence production. No entry plan, readiness score, regulatory clock, or compliance path is valid unless it follows this sequence. The protocol ensures that entry planning is built from the right inputs, in the right order.

Why Sequence Matters

Entry Planning Without Sequence Is Reactive Planning

Most non-EU companies approach European entry by selecting a country first, then discovering regulations, then discovering compliance gaps, then discovering funding requirements — in an order determined by problems encountered, not decisions made. This is reactive planning. It is expensive and preventable.

The EuraPlan Entry Planning Protocol reverses this. It establishes the inputs that must be known before any output — entry plan, compliance path, funding readiness, country selection — can be validly produced.

The Twelve Steps

The Entry Planning Sequence

  1. Identify Company Origin

    Establish the company's country or region of incorporation: United States, United Kingdom, GCC, MENA, Sub-Saharan Africa, South and Southeast Asia, East Asia, Latin America, Canada and Australia. Origin determines regulatory equivalence conditions and funding eligibility.

  2. Identify Entity Type

    Classify the company: SaaS platform, AI system provider, AI system deployer, GPAI model developer, IoT manufacturer, HealthTech company, FinTech company, cybersecurity company, marketplace, startup, scale-up, or enterprise. Entity type determines which EU regulatory frameworks apply.

  3. Identify Product or Service Type

    Establish the specific product or service being brought to market in Europe. The applicable EU regulatory framework is determined at product and service level — not at company level alone.

  4. Map Regulatory Exposure

    Identify which EU regulations apply given entity type, product type, and sector. This is the Regulatory Entry Clock input layer. Common exposure combinations: EU AI Act + GDPR + EU Data Act for AI/SaaS companies. CRA + GDPR + MDR for connected HealthTech devices.

  5. Identify Target EU Country or Countries

    Determine the primary EU entry country based on sector fit, regulatory implementation context, talent ecosystem, customer concentration, and funding environment. Country selection is a planning output, not a starting assumption.

  6. Identify Sector

    Classify the primary sector: AI, SaaS, MedTech, FinTech, CleanTech, Cybersecurity, IoT, Industrial, Marketplace. Sector classification determines sector-specific regulatory frameworks, funding programmes, and partner ecosystems.

  7. Identify Entry Objective

    Establish the primary entry objective: sell product or service, open legal entity, access EU funding or grants, join EU tender or procurement, find EU partners, launch SaaS, hire EU talent, or distribute hardware. The objective determines the entry sequence priority.

  8. Map EU Funding and Tender Relevance

    Assess the company's eligibility for relevant EU funding instruments: Horizon Europe, European Innovation Council (EIC), ERDF, InvestEU, EIC Accelerator, EU procurement and tender frameworks. Funding readiness depends on entity structure, sector, and country.

  9. Score Entry Readiness (EERS)

    Apply the European Entry Readiness Standard across the eight dimensions: Regulatory Mapping, Compliance Timeline, Country Selection, Entity & Legal Structure, Funding Awareness, Product Compliance, Risk Classification, and Execution Plan.

  10. Identify Planning Gaps

    Map the gaps between current readiness state and EERS threshold. Planning gaps become the priority input for the 30/90/180-day entry plan. Gaps that are not identified are gaps that are managed reactively during execution.

  11. Produce 30/90/180-Day Entry Plan

    Build the sequenced entry plan: what must be done in the first 30 days (regulatory mapping, entity setup decision), the first 90 days (compliance path initiation, funding application readiness), and the first 180 days (market activation, compliance milestone achievement, funding submission).

  12. Map Official Sources and Advisory Categories

    Identify the official sources governing the applicable obligations. Identify the advisory categories required: legal, compliance, regulatory, tax, grants, technical. EuraPlan produces planning intelligence — not legal advice. Advisory engagement is a planning output, not an alternative to it.

Protocol Output

What the Protocol Produces

A complete protocol run produces: a regulatory exposure map, a Regulatory Entry Clock for the applicable regulations, an EERS readiness score, a planning gap register, a 30/90/180-day entry plan, and an advisory category map. No intelligence output is valid without a complete protocol run.
