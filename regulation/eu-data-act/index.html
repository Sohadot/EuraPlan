01 - Identity & applicability

Instrument identity & the entry clock

The Data Act is EP-CLM-000046 (Reg (EU) 2023/2854), in force since EP-CLM-000047 and applicable in general from 12 September 2025 (EP-CLM-000048). Three dates gate any entry plan: the EP-CLM-000049 access-by-design duty from 12 Sep 2026, Chapter IV for new contracts from 12 Sep 2025 (EP-CLM-000050) and for legacy indefinite/long contracts from 12 Sep 2027 (EP-CLM-000051).

Decision use

Place each obligation on the clock before committing engineering or contract effort: the design duty and the legacy-contract cut-over are the two dates most often missed.

02 - Who is in scope

Scope, definitions & the size gate

Scope turns on four definitions: connected product EP-CLM-000052, related service EP-CLM-000053, user EP-CLM-000054 and data holder EP-CLM-000055; cloud duties turn on the data-processing-service definition EP-CLM-000056. A microenterprise/small-enterprise carve-out EP-CLM-000057 (extended to recently-medium firms EP-CLM-000058) removes Chapter II duties, subject to its partner/linked/subcontractor conditions. Where personal data is in play, the GDPR/privacy stack prevails on conflict EP-CLM-000059 and a legal basis (GDPR Art 6/9 + ePrivacy Art 5(3)) is required where the user is not the data subject EP-CLM-000060.

Co-render boundary — instrument identity + data-protection prevalence

EP-CLM-000046 — The EU Data Act is Regulation (EU) 2023/2854 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 13 December 2023 (CELEX 32023R2854), a regulation directly applicable in all Member States.

EP-CLM-000059 — Where the Data Act conflicts with Union law on the protection of personal data or privacy, or with national implementing law, the data-protection or privacy law prevails (Article 1(5)). (must render together)

03 - Product data (Chapter II)

User access & third-party sharing

The core Chapter II rights: access-by-design EP-CLM-000062 with pre-contract information EP-CLM-000063/EP-CLM-000064; a data-holder access duty where direct access is not possible EP-CLM-000065, bounded by holder use-limits EP-CLM-000066/EP-CLM-000067 and a graduated trade-secret carve-out EP-CLM-000068; and the user right to have data shared with a third party EP-CLM-000069, with the gatekeeper exclusion EP-CLM-000070, third-party purpose limits EP-CLM-000071 and prohibitions EP-CLM-000072. These rights cannot be contracted away — the Article 7(2) anti-waiver EP-CLM-000061 co-renders with each.

Co-render boundary — third-party sharing right + Chapter II anti-waiver

EP-CLM-000069 — At the user's request, the data holder must make product and related-service data available to a third party without undue delay, of the same quality as available to the data holder and, where relevant and technically feasible, continuously and in real time; trade secrets are preserved and disclosed to the third party only as strictly necessary for the agreed purpose, and where the necessary confidentiality measures are not agreed or are breached the data holder may withhold or suspend, or in exceptional cases refuse, sharing of the identified data (Article 5(1), read with the trade-secret boundary in Article 5(9)-(11)).

EP-CLM-000061 — Any contractual term that, to the detriment of the user, excludes the application of, derogates from or varies the effect of the user's rights under Chapter II is not binding on the user (Article 7(2)). (must render together)

04 - Data-holder availability (Chapter III)

FRAND availability & compensation

Where a holder is obliged to make data available, terms must be FRAND and transparent EP-CLM-000073 and non-discriminatory EP-CLM-000074; compensation must be reasonable EP-CLM-000075 with an SME/non-profit cost cap EP-CLM-000076. Technical protection measures are permitted but may not defeat the user's rights EP-CLM-000077, and a holder may not push data to a recipient absent a user request EP-CLM-000078. The whole chapter is gated by the Article 12(1) applicability rule EP-CLM-000079 and protected by the Article 12(2) anti-waiver EP-CLM-000080 — both co-render with the duties.

Co-render boundary — availability duty + Chapter III applicability gate

EP-CLM-000073 — Where, in business-to-business relations, a data holder is obliged to make data available to a data recipient under Chapter III, it must do so under fair, reasonable and non-discriminatory terms and in a transparent manner (Article 8(1)).

EP-CLM-000079 — The Chapter III obligations on data holders apply where they are obliged by Article 5, or by other Union law or national implementing law, to make data available to a data recipient (Article 12(1)). (must render together)

Co-render boundary — availability duty + Chapter III anti-waiver

EP-CLM-000073 — Where, in business-to-business relations, a data holder is obliged to make data available to a data recipient under Chapter III, it must do so under fair, reasonable and non-discriminatory terms and in a transparent manner (Article 8(1)).

EP-CLM-000080 — A contractual term in a data-sharing agreement that, to the detriment of one party or the user, excludes, derogates from or varies the application of Chapter III is not binding on that party (Article 12(2)). (must render together)

05 - Unfair contract terms (Chapter IV)

Unilaterally-imposed unfair terms

A unilaterally-imposed unfair term is not binding EP-CLM-000081. The regime is defined by a mandatory-law carve-out EP-CLM-000082, the general unfairness test EP-CLM-000083, the always-unfair EP-CLM-000084 and presumed-unfair EP-CLM-000085 lists, the unilateral-imposition test and burden of proof EP-CLM-000086, severability EP-CLM-000087 and the subject-matter/price exclusion EP-CLM-000088. The Article 13(9) anti-waiver EP-CLM-000089 co-renders with the default.

Co-render boundary — unfair-term unenforceability + Chapter IV anti-waiver

EP-CLM-000081 — A contractual term concerning access to and use of data, or liability and remedies for breach or termination of data-related obligations, that has been unilaterally imposed by one enterprise on another is not binding on the latter if it is unfair (Article 13(1)).

EP-CLM-000089 — Parties to a contract covered by Article 13(1) may not exclude the application of Article 13, derogate from it or vary its effects (Article 13(9)). (must render together)

06 - B2G exceptional need (Chapter V)

Government access on exceptional need

On a demonstrated exceptional need, data holders (legal persons other than public bodies) must make data available EP-CLM-000090. Two routes gate the duty: public emergency EP-CLM-000091 and the non-emergency, non-personal-data route EP-CLM-000092, with a micro/small carve-out on the latter EP-CLM-000093. Procedure (EP-CLM-000094/EP-CLM-000095), the compensation split (free in emergency EP-CLM-000096 vs fair compensation EP-CLM-000097) and PSB limits (EP-CLM-000098/EP-CLM-000099) travel with it. Chapter V does not reach criminal/customs/tax activity EP-CLM-000100.

Co-render boundary — B2G duty + Article 16 scope-limit / criminal-customs-tax carve-out

EP-CLM-000090 — Where a public sector body, the Commission, the ECB or a Union body demonstrates an exceptional need under Article 15 to use data to carry out statutory duties in the public interest, data holders that are legal persons (other than public sector bodies) must make that data available on a duly reasoned request (Article 14).

EP-CLM-000100 — Chapter V does not affect other reporting, access or compliance obligations and does not apply to activities for the prevention, investigation, detection or prosecution of criminal or administrative offences, the execution of criminal penalties, or customs or taxation administration (Article 16). (must render together)

07 - Cloud switching (Chapter VI)

Switching & the exit-obstacle regime

Providers must remove obstacles to switching EP-CLM-000101 (responsibilities limited to the source provider EP-CLM-000102). The contract must be written EP-CLM-000103, carry the mandatory terms — 30-day transition, ≤2-month notice, ≥30-day retrieval EP-CLM-000104 — and the customer options / unfeasibility route EP-CLM-000105, with information EP-CLM-000106 and good-faith cooperation EP-CLM-000107. Switching charges fall away from 12 Jan 2027 EP-CLM-000109 (reduced ≤cost until then EP-CLM-000110); functional equivalence EP-CLM-000111, open interfaces EP-CLM-000112 and standards/export EP-CLM-000113 are the technical duties. A bespoke / non-production exemption EP-CLM-000114 co-renders with these.

Co-render boundary — switching duty + Article 31 bespoke / non-production exemption

EP-CLM-000101 — Providers of data processing services must take the measures in Articles 25, 26, 27, 29 and 30 to enable customers to switch to another provider of the same service type or to on-premises ICT infrastructure, and must not impose and must remove obstacles to switching (Article 23).

EP-CLM-000114 — The Article 23(d), 29 and 30(1)/(3) obligations do not apply to bespoke or custom-built services not offered at broad commercial scale (Article 31(1)); the whole of Chapter VI does not apply to non-production services provided for testing and evaluation for a limited period (Article 31(2)); and the provider must disclose which Chapter VI obligations do not apply (Article 31(3)). (must render together)

08 - International access & interoperability (Ch VII/VIII)

Third-country access, in-parallel use & smart contracts

Providers must prevent conflicting third-country governmental access to non-personal data EP-CLM-000115, recognisable only via international agreement EP-CLM-000116 or, absent one, under the Article 32(3)-(5) conditions EP-CLM-000117; the Article 28 transparency duty EP-CLM-000108 co-renders as its companion. Interoperability for in-parallel use reuses the switching duties mutatis mutandis EP-CLM-000118 with a cost-only egress cap EP-CLM-000119. Smart contracts executing data-sharing agreements must meet the five essential requirements and carry an EU declaration of conformity EP-CLM-000120.

Co-render boundary — transparency duty + international-access prevention (N3 companion)

EP-CLM-000108 — Providers must publish and keep up to date, on their websites, the jurisdiction of the ICT infrastructure used and a general description of measures preventing international governmental access to or transfer of non-personal data held in the Union that would conflict with Union or Member-State law, and must list those websites in their contracts (Article 28(1)/(2)).

EP-CLM-000115 — Providers of data processing services must take all adequate technical, organisational and legal measures to prevent international and third-country governmental access to, or transfer of, non-personal data held in the Union where such access or transfer would conflict with Union or Member-State law (Article 32(1)). (must render together)

09 - Enforcement & final provisions (Ch IX-XI)

Authorities, penalties & savings

Member States designate competent authorities and a data coordinator EP-CLM-000121; GDPR SAs/EDPS supervise personal-data aspects EP-CLM-000122. A non-EU entrant must appoint a legal representative — the enforcement hook — or fall under the competence of all Member States EP-CLM-000123; establishment/competence follows main establishment EP-CLM-000124. Complaint and judicial-remedy rights apply EP-CLM-000125. Penalties must be effective/proportionate/dissuasive (notified by 12 Sep 2025) EP-CLM-000126, with GDPR Art 83 fines for Ch II/III/V EP-CLM-000127 and EDPS fines for Ch V EP-CLM-000128. The sui generis DB right is carved out EP-CLM-000129; sectoral/data-space EP-CLM-000131, pre-2024 EP-CLM-000130 and research EP-CLM-000132 savings bound the general layer.

Entry-risk read

For a non-EU company, EP-CLM-000123 (legal representative) and EP-CLM-000127/EP-CLM-000128 (fine exposure) are the two enforcement inputs that most change an entry decision.
