EU AI Act Entry Planning Reference

For non-EU AI, SaaS, and technology companies entering Europe, the EU AI Act is not merely a regulation to read. It is an entry planning clock — determining which obligations may apply, when they apply, and which role your company occupies in the AI value chain before irreversible market moves.

The AI Act establishes horizontal rules for AI systems and general-purpose AI (GPAI) models in the EU. For European entry planning, it matters because non-EU companies frequently trigger obligations when they place AI on the EU market, put AI into service in the Union, import, distribute, deploy, or provide GPAI models — depending on role, system classification, and intended purpose. EuraPlan maps these as planning gates, not as automatic legal conclusions for any reader.

Entry Planning Problem

Why the EU AI Act Matters for Non-EU Companies

A non-EU company may need to map AI Act exposure before entering the EU market, placing an AI system on the EU market, putting it into service in the Union, distributing or importing it, deploying it in a professional context, or providing a GPAI model where the regulation applies — each trigger is fact-specific.

Europe punishes absence: operating in or toward the EU without mapping role, system scope, and Article 113 phases creates planning gaps that compound into enforcement exposure. Europe rewards planning: mapping the clock early sequences documentation, governance, product, and advisory work before deadlines narrow the execution window. This reference node supports that mapping — it does not certify compliance or assign a final role to your company.

Role Mapping Layer

AI Value Chain Roles — Planning Gates

EuraPlan treats role identification as a planning gate because obligations depend on the role a company occupies in the AI value chain. The list below describes planning relevance — not a determination that any reader holds a specific role.

Provider

Develops an AI system or has one developed and places it on the market or puts it into service under its name or trademark. Planning must map high-risk classification, conformity, and documentation obligations where applicable.

Deployer

Uses an AI system under its authority except for personal non-professional use. Planning must map deployer-specific obligations, human oversight, and use-context — distinct from provider obligations.

Importer

Places on the Union market an AI system bearing the name or trademark of a natural or legal person established outside the Union. Entry planning must sequence import responsibility against provider arrangements.

Distributor

Makes an AI system available on the Union market without being provider or importer. Distribution chains affect where planning responsibility sits — requires mapping, not assumption.

Product manufacturer

Where AI is embedded in a product, manufacturer and product-legislation interactions may affect planning — especially where Annex I harmonisation legislation applies on a later Article 113(4) timeline. AI-enabled products with digital elements may also require parallel CRA product-security mapping.

Authorised representative

Non-EU providers may require EU-established authorised representative planning before market placement — a structural gate in entity and legal planning.

GPAI model provider

Provides a general-purpose AI model. Article 113(2) brings GPAI-related obligations and governance framework elements into application on a distinct phase date from full high-risk provider obligations.

Role definitions above are planning summaries. Authoritative definitions appear in the official regulation text (EUR-Lex). A company may occupy more than one role across different products or EU activities — company-specific mapping requires protocol inputs, not this reference page alone.

High-Risk Planning Layer

High-Risk Classification as a Planning Layer

High-risk analysis is a planning layer, not a slogan. A company must understand whether its system, intended purpose, sector context, and role place it near high-risk obligations under Annex III or Annex I pathways before entering Europe. High-risk classification can materially affect documentation, conformity assessment planning, human oversight design, and sequencing relative to Article 113(3) and 113(4) dates.

EuraPlan does not classify any reader's system on this page. Not every AI product is high-risk. Not every SaaS feature using AI triggers the same obligation set. Classification is a gated planning step tied to product mapping and official text — typically requiring qualified regulatory counsel alongside EuraPlan intelligence outputs.

Entry Planning Implications

What to Plan Before EU Market Entry

Non-EU AI, SaaS, and technology companies typically need to plan the following intelligence layers before irreversible EU moves — without assuming a single compliance outcome:

  • Role mapping Provider, deployer, importer, distributor, GPAI provider — mapped per product and EU activity, not assumed from incorporation country.
  • System & product mapping Which offerings place AI on the EU market or put AI into service; whether features are standalone AI systems or embedded components.
  • Intended purpose mapping Annex III and use-case context drive high-risk proximity — requires product-level analysis, not marketing labels.
  • Timeline mapping Article 113 phases applied to your obligation set — integrated with the Regulatory Entry Clock.
  • Documentation & governance planning Technical documentation, risk management, transparency, and human oversight planning paths where applicable — sequenced, not ad hoc.
  • GDPR coordination Personal data processing for AI features remains governed by GDPR where applicable — parallel planning track via the GDPR Entry Planning Reference, not replaced by AI Act mapping.
  • Country & market sequencing First EU member state, national competent authorities, and enforcement context — via entry gateway country gate (e.g. Germany when in scope).
  • Advisory categories Legal, compliance, regulatory, and technical advisors identified in protocol step 12 — planning output, not substitute for counsel.
  • Future diagnostic outputs Phase 3 diagnostic engine (planned) will apply protocol inputs to governed outputs — this reference page remains the static ontology node.
Cross-Regulation Coordination

EU AI Act + GDPR Planning Coordination

Where AI systems process personal data, the AI Act and GDPR require separate planning tracks. AI Act mapping addresses provider/deployer roles, risk classification, and Article 113 phases. GDPR mapping addresses controller/processor roles, data flows, transfers, and baseline accountability — already active since 2018. Where operational or product data involves access, sharing, or switching architecture, Data Act mapping may run as a parallel track. AI-enabled products with digital elements may also require CRA product-security mapping — a separate track from AI Act risk classification.

EuraPlan does not merge these frameworks into a single checklist. The Entry Planning Protocol captures each at regulatory exposure mapping (step 04) where product context requires them.
